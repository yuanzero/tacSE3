03 / RESEARCH OVERVIEW

Motion feedback from touch

When an object is held inside a gripper, occlusion and a lack of surface texture can make visual motion tracking unreliable. TacSE3 uses the deformation measured by two opposing visuotactile fingertips to recover short-horizon contact motion.

The method builds a calibrated 3D surface-deformation field. Contact-centroid motion supplies planar translation, while spatial variations in tangential deformation support a rigid-body rotation fit. The two sensor estimates are expressed in the end-effector frame and fused. Normal deformation contributes to contact geometry; independent object translation along the normal is constrained by the opposing fingers.

The paper evaluates motion estimation, translation–rotation coupling, changes in robot pose and contact location, different object geometries, and execution-time compensation with a frozen policy.

Method Overview

Figure 1: Overview of the proposed tactile-image-to-SE(3) estimation pipeline. Given a sequence of tactile observations, the method identifies the active contact region, maps the tactile response to a calibrated 3D deformation field, fits a rigid-body model in SE(3), and integrates the estimated twist for online compensation.


TacSE3 treats the tactile image as a geometric measurement of contact rather than a conventional textured image. The pipeline follows three principles: contact-first sensing, deformation-field decomposition, and rigid-body consistency. Planar translation is derived from the contact-mask centroid, while rotation is estimated from the shear-related field — avoiding ill-conditioned coupling in a single joint fit.

Motivation

Even when an object is initially grasped securely, small translations, rolling motions, or local rotations relative to the gripper can accumulate into alignment errors and task failure. TacSE3 detects and compensates for in-gripper object drift in real time using only tactile sensing.

The challenge is especially severe for low-texture objects (polished spheres, smooth cylinders) where vision-based tracking fails and traditional tactile methods relying on image correspondences break down. TacSE3 addresses this by treating the tactile stream as a geometric measurement of surface deformation rather than a textured image, enabling constrained SE(3) motion estimation under textureless contact conditions.
